# Incident Reporting Policy

## 1. Purpose and situations to report

This internal policy of Atlas Services, a fictional Moroccan company, explains how to report a suspected IT incident. It covers suspicious messages, unusual access, lost equipment, and accidental disclosures. Employees report suspicious situations as soon as they notice them, even if the incident is still unconfirmed.

## 2. Reporting procedure

Employees use the designated internal IT Security channel. They give the time of the observation, the facts observed, and the affected service or equipment. They describe any steps already taken. The line manager helps relay the report when needed.

| Observed situation | Useful information | Initial precaution |
|---|---|---|
| Suspicious message | Time and context of receipt | Avoid clicking links or forwarding the message to the team |
| Lost equipment | Type of equipment and last known location | Report the loss promptly |
| Accidental disclosure | Channel, time, and data category | Avoid redistributing the affected data |

## 3. Initial triage

IT Security receives the report, assesses impact and urgency, and tells the reporter what to do next. The reporter communicates newly observed facts. IT Security leads the investigation.

## 4. Evidence preservation

Employees preserve available original evidence and use approved channels to send it to IT Security. They limit copies, follow data handling rules, and avoid deleting or altering evidence related to the report.

**Conditional approval.** If restoring service could alter evidence, the employee must obtain IT Security approval before acting.

## 5. Exceptions and escalation

**Exception.** If the designated channel is unavailable, the employee alerts their line manager through another approved internal channel so the manager can relay the report promptly to IT Security.

The employee reports any change that increases impact or urgency through the available approved channel. Investigation details remain with IT Security.
